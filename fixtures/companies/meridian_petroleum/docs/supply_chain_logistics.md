# Meridian Petroleum — Supply Chain & Logistics

## Crude feedstock slate

The Gulf Coast refinery is configured to run a flexible slate. Today the mix is
roughly:

- **38% sour Middle East crude** — competitively priced, but routed through the
  **Strait of Hormuz** and therefore the most geopolitically exposed barrels we buy.
- **44% domestic / WTI** — pipeline-delivered, the most supply-secure portion.
- **18% West African and other waterborne** — diversification barrels we are growing.

**Strategic goal:** cut the Hormuz-dependent share from **38% to under 25%** by
qualifying incremental **West African (WAF)** and **WTI** feedstock. The refinery has
confirmed the slate can swing; the constraint is qualification lead time and the
turnaround schedule.

## Inbound shipping

Waterborne crude arrives on **VLCCs** (very large crude carriers). The two cargoes
inbound through Hormuz this month are the live exposure: a Strait-of-Hormuz threat
spikes **war-risk insurance premiums** (recently up ~5x overnight) and **VLCC day
rates**, and can force a **diversion** that delays feedstock to the refinery.

We monitor tanker-market signals continuously — day rates, war-risk premiums, and
operator commentary (e.g., Frontline) — because freight is a real and volatile line
in the delivered cost of every sour barrel.

## Refinery operations

- **Capacity:** 185,000 bpd; **throughput:** ~171,000 bpd.
- **Uptime:** ~97%, the license to operate. Process safety is non-negotiable.
- **Turnaround:** a planned unit turnaround is in progress and must finish on
  schedule and incident-free even as crude volatility pulls at the maintenance budget.

## Outbound products & logistics

Refined products (gasoline, diesel, jet) move to wholesale racks, branded and
unbranded retail, and commercial offtakers (trucking fleets, regional airlines) via
pipeline and truck. Supply reliability to diesel and jet buyers through a
geopolitical disruption is a core commercial commitment.

## Single-source / single-lane risk

The operating principle: **single-lane feedstock dependence is a risk to retire, not
a relationship to defend.** Hormuz concentration is the current example — the
diversification initiative exists precisely so that one strait closure or insurance
shock cannot strand the refinery.
