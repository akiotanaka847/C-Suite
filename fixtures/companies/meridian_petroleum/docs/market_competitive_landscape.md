# Meridian Petroleum — Market & Competitive Landscape

## Who sets our prices

Meridian is a price-taker on crude and a margin-maker on products. The entities that
set the terms we live under:

- **OPEC+ / Saudi Aramco** — set the crude supply ceiling and spare capacity. Their
  response to a supply shock decides whether a price spike is brief or structural.
- **The Iran conflict** — the dominant near-term swing factor on both price (war
  premium on Brent) and supply (Strait of Hormuz shipping risk).
- **Global trading houses** (**Vitol**, **Trafigura**, **Glencore**) — out-scale our
  desk and move physical cargoes globally; we compete on discipline and intelligence,
  not volume.

## Refiner peers (the margin benchmark)

We benchmark refining margin and crack spreads against the large independents and
integrateds:

- **Valero (VLO)** — the scale benchmark on the Gulf Coast.
- **Marathon Petroleum (MPC)** — integrated refining + retail (Speedway) footprint.
- **Phillips 66 (PSX)** — integrated downstream and midstream peer.
- **PBF Energy (PBF)** — independent refiner with a comparable margin profile and
  similar geopolitical exposure.

When any peer reports crack-spread compression or a feedstock shift, it is a
read-through on our own margin and worth a same-week look.

## Where Meridian competes

We are too small to win on volume against the majors or the global traders. We win on:

- **Slate flexibility** — swinging between sour Middle East and light WTI/WAF crude
  faster than a single-configuration refiner.
- **Risk discipline** — a hedging book and VaR limits that keep us solvent and supplied
  through the shocks that strand less-prepared competitors.
- **Clean-cargo reputation** — a conservative sanctions posture that keeps us a
  trustworthy counterparty when others get caught holding tainted barrels.
- **Intelligence speed** — reading the geopolitical and freight signals before they
  move the price, and routing each to the owner who acts on it.

## Demand side

Demand for our refined products (gasoline, diesel, jet) is price-inelastic in the
short run but **politically sensitive**: pump prices become a headline whenever crude
spikes, which is why pricing communications and crisis comms are a first-class
function, not an afterthought.
