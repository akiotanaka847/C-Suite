# Meridian Petroleum — Financials & Risk

## How a refiner makes money

Meridian earns the **refining margin** (crack spread) — the difference between what
we pay for crude and what we sell refined products for — plus trading-desk P&L. The
north-star metric is **refining margin per barrel, net of hedging**. It currently
sits at **~$14.20/bbl** against an **$18.00** target; a crude spike that we cannot
immediately pass through is what compresses it.

## The hedging book

We **take price risk on purpose, never by accident.** The hedging program uses
**collars, swaps, and options** to cap catastrophic downside on margin without giving
up all upside.

- **Q3 crude hedged share:** ~**45%**, laddering toward a **70%** target as the
  Iran-conflict volatility plays out.
- **Policy:** any **unhedged crude exposure above the desk limit** escalates to the
  principal the **same day**.
- **Mechanics:** the desk uses spikes to take profit on existing collars and roll into
  the next tranche rather than trying to time the war.

## Trading-desk value-at-risk (VaR)

The crude and refined-products desk runs to a board-approved **VaR limit of $10M**.
Current VaR is **~$9.5M** — close to the line during the volatility spike, which is
why the limit review and stress scenarios (including a Strait-of-Hormuz-closure tail
case) are an active initiative.

A crude spike can drive a **mark-to-market loss** on near-dated paper and trigger
**margin calls** from counterparties; treasury must be ready to post margin or the
desk must trim the position to stay inside the limit.

## Risk posture

The dominant near-term risk is the **Iran conflict** transmitting into our P&L via
crude price, Hormuz shipping, and sanctions. We manage it with three levers —
**hedging** (Finance), **feedstock diversification** (Operations), and **disciplined
pass-through pricing** (Marketing/Commercial) — rather than betting the balance sheet
on the conflict resolving a particular way.
