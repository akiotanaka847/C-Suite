# Tandem Robotics — Company Overview

**Tandem Robotics** is a US-based humanoid-robotics startup founded in 2020, now
at Series C with roughly 420 employees and about $90M in recurring revenue. Our
product is a general-purpose warehouse humanoid leased as a service: customers pay
on **uptime and billable robot-hours**, not a multi-million-dollar purchase.

## The thesis

Mid-market warehouses are not waiting for a more dexterous robot. They are waiting
for one they can actually deploy — without eight-figure capex, a year-long
integration, or an in-house robotics team — that shows up and works a full shift.
Every incumbent automation play targeting this buyer still demands huge fixed
infrastructure or only pencils out at Amazon scale. Tandem's bet is to make the
robot a **service priced against labor cost-per-pick**, and to win on **uptime**,
not on demo dexterity.

## What makes Tandem different

- **Robots-as-a-Service.** Customers pay opex on an uptime SLA and billable
  robot-hours — no capex, no stranded asset, upgrade path on us.
- **Deploy-in-a-day.** The robot works in human-designed aisles with no fixed
  infrastructure or re-racking, so a 50-person 3PL can start in days, not quarters.
- **Autonomy with teleop fallback.** Fleet autonomy plus remote-teleop fallback
  means the customer needs no robotics or ML staff to run it.
- **Reliability-first engineering.** We optimize autonomy rate and uptime over
  flashy manipulation, because renewals turn on robot-hours billed.

## Where we are

- The billable deployed fleet is growing from ~**380** toward a target of **600
  robots**, with a **24-site** pilot pipeline to convert.
- Fleet autonomy rate is **~82%** (target **95%**); every teleop intervention is
  cost-to-serve.
- Gross margin per deployed robot is currently **negative (~-9%)**; reaching
  gross-margin-positive before the next raise is the year's north-star metric.
- Runway is roughly **16 months** at ~$14M/month burn.

## The competitive frame

We do not try to beat Tesla Optimus on vertical integration or Boston Dynamics on
R&D. We win on **deploy-in-a-day**, **uptime SLA**, and **cost-per-pick versus
labor** against Figure, Agility Robotics (Digit), and Apptronik (Apollo) — the
rivals actually contesting real warehouse deployments. The looming structural
threat is **Unitree/UBTech** — currently export-control and tariff-gated, but the
global cost leaders that set the long-run RaaS price ceiling.

## Why external monitoring matters here

This is a sector where the news *is* the strategy. A Figure deployment win, an
export-control ruling on rare-earth magnets, a new tariff on China-sourced
actuators, an OSHA workplace-safety rule for shared human-robot floors, or a
competitor's aggressive RaaS pricing can each change the deployment calculus within
a week. Tandem tracks these signals continuously and routes them to the department
head who owns the response.
