# Halcyon Motors — GTM & Marketing

Owner: **Morgan Patel (Head of Marketing & People)**

## Positioning
Halcyon is the **affordable first EV for mainstream households** — the car you trade
into from an aging gas crossover, not the one you aspire to after a luxury sedan.
The brand voice is transparent and value-driven, never luxury-tier.

## The core narrative: total cost of ownership
Our central marketing asset is a **transparent total-cost-of-ownership (TCO) story**
comparing the C1 against the **Tesla Model Y** and **Chevy Equinox EV** — purchase
price, charging cost (with NACS Supercharger access), insurance, and expected
service. We sell the math, not the spec sheet.

## Funnel
- Direct-to-consumer reservation funnel (no dealers).
- Current blended customer acquisition cost: ~$1,650 per reservation (target:
  under $1,400).
- Reservation growth: ~+11% QoQ (target: +20%).

## Competitive messaging response
Because rival incentive and price moves shift our funnel directly, Marketing keeps a
**rapid-response playbook**:
- A **Tesla Model Y price cut** → publish/refresh the TCO comparison rather than cut
  price; coordinate the line with Strategy.
- A **competitor charging announcement** (e.g., Ford/GM/Polestar NACS milestones) →
  reinforce our day-one Supercharger-access claim.
- An **EV tax-credit change** → update all on-site pricing math the same day, since
  the effective price to the customer changes.

## Signals Marketing watches
- Competitor price cuts, incentives, and lease deals (Tesla, GM, Hyundai/Kia, Ford).
- Competitor brand campaigns and messaging shifts.
- Consumer EV-sentiment and range-anxiety coverage in mainstream press.
- Tax-credit and incentive news that changes the customer's effective price.

When a signal warrants a customer-facing response, the recommendation routes to
Marketing; pricing decisions are escalated to Strategy/Finance first.
