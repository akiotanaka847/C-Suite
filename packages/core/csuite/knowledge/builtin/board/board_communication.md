# Board Communication and Governance

## The Purpose of a Board Meeting

A board meeting is not a status update. It is a governance mechanism where:
1. The board fulfills its fiduciary duty (oversight of management)
2. Management accesses the board's experience and networks
3. Critical decisions requiring board approval are made

The worst board meetings are those where management presents everything that happened since the last meeting and the board asks clarifying questions. The best board meetings focus on 2-3 strategic decisions where the board's input is genuinely valuable.

## Board Deck Structure

**Standard structure (2-hour meeting)**:

1. **Executive Summary** (5 min): 1 page. The 3-4 things the board most needs to understand coming into this meeting. Not a recap — the CEO's point of view on the state of the business.

2. **Business Update** (20-30 min): Financials vs. plan, key metrics vs. prior period, progress against strategic priorities. Include: what's working, what's not, what changed since last board meeting.

3. **Deep Dive Topic 1** (30-40 min): Pre-selected strategic topic where you want board input. Examples: M&A opportunity, market expansion decision, fundraising strategy, organizational change.

4. **Deep Dive Topic 2** (20-30 min): Second strategic topic.

5. **Decisions Required** (10-15 min): Any items requiring formal board approval (material contracts, equity grants, etc.).

6. **Executive Session** (15 min): CEO leaves, board discusses management performance.

**Pre-read**: Send the full deck 5-7 days in advance. Board members who haven't read the materials are a boardroom problem, not a presentation problem. Start the meeting assuming they've read it.

## Communicating Bad News

Board members hate surprises more than bad news. The rules:

1. **Call before the meeting**: Material negative developments (missed quarter, losing a key customer, executive departure) warrant a phone call to board members before they see it in a deck.

2. **Lead with the problem, not the context**: "We missed Q2 revenue by 22%. Here is what happened and what we're doing about it." Not three slides of context before the number.

3. **Show you understand the root cause**: Don't present a miss without a credible diagnosis of why. "Revenue was light because our SMB segment churned faster than expected. Here is why churn accelerated [specific analysis] and what we're changing."

4. **Present a plan, not just the problem**: Boards have low tolerance for problems presented without a solution under development. "We don't know yet" is acceptable if you explain your process for finding out. "We'll figure it out" is not.

5. **Quantify the path back**: How does the Q2 miss affect annual guidance? What is the updated scenario range?

## Investor Updates (Between Board Meetings)

Monthly investor updates for active investors are a strong practice. They:
- Maintain investor confidence between board meetings
- Reduce the amount of board meeting time spent on updates
- Create a communication pattern that makes bad news delivery easier

Template:
- **Highlights**: 3 bullet points on what went well
- **Lowlights**: 2-3 bullet points on what is challenging (include this — investors who only see highlights become suspicious)
- **Key metrics**: 5-8 metrics with trend vs. prior period
- **Ask**: What you need from investors this month (intros, candidates, advice)

Keep it short — ideally one scrollable email. The investors who read it most carefully are the most engaged ones; don't punish them with length.

## Board Governance Basics

**Board composition for VC-backed companies (typical)**:
- Seed/Series A: 2 founders, 1 lead investor, 2 independent directors
- Series B+: May add additional investor seats, typically stay at 5-7 total members

**Independent directors**: Valuable when chosen well. The best independent directors bring: a specific expertise gap (former CMO if you need marketing credibility), an important network (customer relationships, future investors), or governance experience (former board member at comparable companies).

**Fiduciary duties**: Board members have a fiduciary duty to the corporation (not to any one class of shareholders). When interests of common and preferred diverge (as in a sale process), this becomes legally significant.

**Executive session**: Every board meeting should end with an executive session — CEO and other executives exit, board discusses management performance and sensitive matters. This is healthy governance, not a sign of distrust.
