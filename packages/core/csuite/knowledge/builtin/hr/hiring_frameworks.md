# Executive and Team Hiring Frameworks

## Defining the Role Before Hiring

The most common hiring mistake: posting a job description before defining what success looks like in the role.

Before writing the JD, define:
1. **The problem this person solves**: What will be measurably different 12 months after a great hire?
2. **The scorecard** (not the job description): 3-5 outcomes the person must achieve, ordered by importance
3. **The competencies** that predict success in those outcomes — not a skills checklist, the specific behaviors needed for this role, at this company, at this stage
4. **The culture fit test**: 2-3 behaviors that are dealbreakers regardless of competence

Example scorecard (VP Sales):
- Within 90 days: Rebuilt sales process with clear stages and CRM discipline; pipeline is at 3x quota
- Within 6 months: Quota attainment across AEs > 75%; hired 2 additional AEs
- Within 12 months: ARR contribution of $3M; sales cycle compressed by 20%; NPS from new customers > 50

## Structured Interviewing

Unstructured interviews select for people who are good at interviews. Structured interviews predict performance.

**Behavioral questions**: "Tell me about a time when..." — assess past behavior as a predictor of future behavior. Ask for:
- Specific situation (not hypothetical)
- Their specific actions (not the team's)
- The outcome (with numbers when possible)
- What they would do differently

**Challenge the narrative**: Don't accept the first answer. Probe: "What specifically did you do?" "What was the hardest part?" "What would your manager say about that?"

**Topgrading A-Player Method**:
For final candidates, conduct a chronological work history interview covering every role:
- Why did you take this role?
- What were you hired to accomplish?
- What were your biggest successes? (get specifics)
- What were your biggest failures? (watch for accountability vs. blame)
- Why did you leave?
- What will [your manager] say were your strengths and weaknesses when we call them?

That last question — asking what their reference will say — produces honest answers because they know you'll verify.

## Reference Checks

Reference checks are the most underused tool in hiring. Rules:
1. Call references they didn't give you (find them on LinkedIn). Former colleagues are more candid than designated references.
2. Ask: "On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate their performance?" Anything below 8 is worth probing. "What would make them a 10?"
3. Ask: "Would you hire them again, without hesitation?" Yes/no. No ambiguity.
4. "What is the environment in which they do their best work?" (Reveals fit)
5. "What are their greatest weaknesses?" (References will usually tell you if you ask directly)

## Executive Hiring Specifics

Hire executives 6 months before you need them. The candidate market for great executives is never available on demand.

For C-suite hires:
- The CEO must personally recruit — not outsource to recruiting firms for the sourcing, only for the process
- Use your investor network and board to source — warm introductions outperform cold outreach 3:1
- Reference check the reference checkers: call people who know the candidates who aren't on their list

The onboarding of an executive determines whether the hire succeeds. Build a 90-day plan together before day one: what they need to learn, who they need to meet, and what decisions they own vs. advise on in the first 30/60/90 days.

## Common Hiring Mistakes

1. **Hiring for past stage, not current stage**: Someone who scaled a company from $50M to $500M may not be right for $5M to $50M. Competencies required are different.

2. **Speed over quality**: Filling a role 30 days faster with a B-player costs you 18 months of underperformance and the pain of managing them out.

3. **Culture as code for homogeneity**: "Culture fit" should mean shared values and ways of working, not same background, personality type, or communication style.

4. **Skipping the work sample**: Have candidates do a relevant exercise (a financial analysis, a product critique, a sales call simulation). Reveals ability and reveals how they handle ambiguity.
