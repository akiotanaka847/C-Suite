# Go-To-Market Strategy

## GTM Foundation: ICP First

Before channel strategy, you need a precise Ideal Customer Profile (ICP). Vague ICPs produce vague GTM. A good ICP specifies:

- **Firmographics**: industry, company size (employees or revenue), geography, tech stack (for SaaS)
- **Situation**: growth stage, funding status, trigger event that makes them ready to buy
- **Pain**: the specific problem they have, in their words (use language from your best customer conversations)
- **Internal champion**: their role/title, what they care about, how they are measured
- **Economic buyer**: who approves budget, what outcomes matter to them

Your ICP should be narrow enough that you can name 50 companies that fit it. If you can name 50,000, it's not specific enough.

## The GTM Motions

**Product-Led Growth (PLG)**
- Users find, adopt, and expand the product with minimal sales involvement
- Works when: low price point, immediate individual value, virality within organizations
- Requires: excellent onboarding, in-product conversion triggers, usage data for expansion signals
- Examples: Slack, Figma, Notion

**Sales-Led Growth (SLG)**
- Sales team drives adoption, typically top-down with economic buyer
- Works when: high ACV, complex buying committee, significant onboarding/implementation
- Requires: outbound/inbound pipeline, clear sales process, SE support for enterprise
- Examples: Salesforce, Workday

**Community-Led Growth**
- Users help each other, create content, refer organically
- Works when: strong practitioner identity, clear use case community exists
- Requires: dedicated community resources, platform, strong initial seed community
- Examples: HubSpot User Groups, dbt Community

**Partner-Led Growth**
- Resellers, ISVs, system integrators, agencies drive distribution
- Works when: your product is adjacent to a high-touch service, or embedded in another workflow
- Requires: partner economics (20-30% margin to make it worth their while), partner enablement, co-selling motion

Most successful companies combine motions at different stages: PLG at the bottom, SLG for upmarket expansion.

## Channel Economics

**Paid Acquisition**
- Track CAC by channel (don't blend)
- Paid search: high intent, competitive pricing, scales with budget but CAC tends to rise over time
- Paid social: awareness/consideration, lower intent, useful for brand and top-of-funnel
- Content syndication: variable quality, test carefully

**Organic / Content**
- Lower CAC in steady state, but 6-18 month payback on content investment
- SEO: match content to search intent — bottom-of-funnel (product/solution terms) first, then top-of-funnel
- Thought leadership: effective for establishing category credibility, hard to attribute directly to pipeline

**Outbound**
- High cost per touch, scales with headcount, but allows precise ICP targeting
- Personalization is the differentiator — spray-and-pray SDR models are broken
- Trigger-based outbound (funding announcements, leadership changes, job postings) outperforms cold by 3-5x

## Pricing Strategy

**Value-based pricing**: What is the ROI of your product? Price at a fraction of the value delivered.
**Competitor-based pricing**: Benchmark to alternatives, position above or below based on your differentiation.
**Cost-plus pricing**: Dangerous for SaaS — your costs are mostly fixed, so this tends to undervalue the product.

Common SaaS pricing structures:
- Per seat (usage scales with value)
- Usage-based (aligns cost with consumption — friendly to buyers, creates revenue unpredictability)
- Flat fee (simple to sell, hard to expand)
- Tiered (Good/Better/Best — most common, enables land-and-expand)

**Pricing is not a set-and-forget decision.** Review annually. Price increases of 5-10% annually are sustainable for strong products and materially impact revenue.

## Launch Planning

A product launch is not a day — it's a 6-week campaign.

Pre-launch (4 weeks out):
- Align with key customers for testimonials and case studies
- Brief friendly press
- Seed communities and influencers
- Build waitlist/pre-registration if applicable

Launch week:
- Press coverage (earned, not paid)
- Product Hunt if relevant
- Email to full list
- Social campaign
- Customer announcements

Post-launch (2 weeks):
- Follow-up content (learnings, customer stories)
- Retarget engaged visitors
- Sales team enablement on launch messaging
