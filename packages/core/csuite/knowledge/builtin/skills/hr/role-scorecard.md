# Role Scorecard

A scorecard defines success in the role BEFORE the job description is written. It is the single most underused hiring document.

## Output structure

Produce a one-page scorecard with five sections, in this order.

### 1. Role purpose (1-2 sentences)

The problem this role solves for the company. NOT the responsibilities. The reason the role exists.

Example: "Build the marketing function that takes us from founder-led sales to a repeatable inbound + outbound pipeline engine generating 60% of new ARR."

### 2. Outcomes (3-5, ordered by importance)

Specific, measurable results the person must produce in the first 12 months. Each outcome:
- Has a metric or visible deliverable
- Has a timeframe
- Is the primary accountability of this role, not shared

Example outcomes for a VP Marketing:
- *By month 3*: Marketing function stood up with first hire in demand gen, brand book delivered, weekly metrics review live with sales
- *By month 6*: $2M of qualified pipeline created, marketing-sourced; demand gen channels tested across 4 sources with CPL benchmarks
- *By month 12*: Marketing contributing 50% of net-new ARR; CAC payback in target range; brand recognition measurable in target accounts

### 3. Competencies (3-5, with behavioral indicators)

Not a skills checklist. The 3-5 capabilities most predictive of success in THIS role at THIS stage, with behavioral indicators of what "great" looks like.

Example:
- *Builder mentality* — has built marketing functions from <5 people to >25, named the playbooks they brought, shipped systems that outlasted their tenure
- *Data orientation* — runs marketing as an instrumented system; can explain attribution, funnel math, CAC payback unprompted; comfortable with SQL or analytics tooling
- *Sales partnership* — has shipped SLAs with sales, attended QBRs, run joint campaigns; can name the sales leaders they worked with

### 4. Culture fit / non-negotiables (2-3)

The 2-3 behaviors that are dealbreakers regardless of competence. Tied to the company's values, not abstract platitudes.

Example:
- Operates with urgency and transparent reporting — not perfectionism that delays shipping
- Disagrees and commits — argues their position in meetings, then executes the decided plan
- Hires above their bar — names hires they brought in who are now stronger than them

### 5. 30/60/90 day plan (a brief sketch)

What the person should be focused on in their first three months. Builds in time for learning before judgment.

- *First 30*: meet team, customers, partners; understand metrics; produce a written assessment of the current state
- *First 60*: propose a plan for the function; first hire; first quick win shipped
- *First 90*: plan approved and executing; team aligned; outcomes from section 2 underway

## Tone and discipline

- Write outcomes the candidate could be evaluated on objectively
- No platitudes ("must be passionate") — replace with behaviors
- Reuse this scorecard during interviews — every interview question maps to an outcome or competency
- After 90 days, review against the scorecard — most "bad hire" decisions are actually "bad scorecard" decisions caught late

## What NOT to include

- Responsibilities list (use the JD for that)
- Skills checklist disconnected from outcomes
- Generic culture statements ("we value integrity")
- Anything that would be true of any senior hire at any company
