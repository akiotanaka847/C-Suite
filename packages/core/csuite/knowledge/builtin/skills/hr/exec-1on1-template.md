# Executive 1:1 Template

The 1:1 is the highest-leverage 30 minutes in management. Most are run badly because they default to status updates. This template separates the recurring, the rotating, and the strategic — so the time actually moves the relationship and the work.

## Inputs to gather first

Before applying the template, confirm:

1. **The relationship** — CEO ↔ functional exec, exec ↔ direct report, etc.
2. **The cadence** — weekly (default), biweekly, or other
3. **The duration** — 30 minutes is standard; 45-60 for biweekly or new reports
4. **Current pain points** — is the relationship working? What's missing?

If the 1:1 is being launched for the first time, the first 1-2 should be longer (60+ min) to establish patterns.

## Output structure

The template has four sections. Total time: 30 minutes.

### Section 1: Open (3 minutes)

*"How are you?"*

Not perfunctory. Real check-in. The 1:1 is also a relationship — and relationships need this beat.

- Energy and well-being
- Workload reality
- Anything outside work affecting the week

If the answer is "busy / fine", the relationship hasn't been built yet. That's the work to do.

### Section 2: Their agenda (15 minutes)

*"What's on your mind?"*

The report drives. Manager prompts and listens.

Common topics:
- Priority work for the week
- Decisions they need (their decision; your input)
- Blockers (your help requested)
- Cross-functional friction
- People issues on their team
- Strategic questions about the function
- Career and development

**Discipline:**
- The report owns the agenda; share a running doc both can edit
- Manager doesn't take over — coaching questions, not statements
- If the report has nothing to discuss, you have a different problem (lack of trust, lack of safety, or actually nothing — which is unusual)

**Coaching questions (instead of giving the answer):**
- "What have you tried?"
- "What's the trade-off you're navigating?"
- "What would you do if I weren't available?"
- "What does your gut say?"
- "What's the risk if we go your way?"

### Section 3: My agenda (10 minutes)

*"Here are a few things from my side."*

The manager's topics. Includes:

**Recurring (every 1:1):**
- Feedback (both reinforcing and corrective; specific to last week)
- Strategic context that affects their work
- Decisions or priorities from above that they need to know

**Rotating (every 4-6 weeks):**
- Career growth conversation — what's next for them, what they want, what skills to build
- Calibration on their team — high performers, concerns, hiring needs
- Performance reflection — how I think they're doing; what I'd raise the bar on
- Skip-level signals — what I'm hearing from their team

**Strategic (every 4-6 weeks):**
- Cross-functional alignment topics
- Big-picture company direction relevant to them
- Strategic bets being made in their area

### Section 4: Close (2 minutes)

*"What are the action items, and when's our next 1:1?"*

- Action items captured in the shared doc (who, what, by when)
- Confirm next 1:1 timing
- Any follow-ups needed before next 1:1 (escalations, decisions you said you'd make)

## Recurring topics across all 1:1s

Things that should be in the cycle (rotating, not every week):

- **Goals / OKR status** — every 2-4 weeks (more during planning quarters)
- **Performance / feedback** — every 1-2 weeks (reinforcing); whenever needed (corrective)
- **Career growth** — every 4-6 weeks
- **Team and people topics** — every 1-2 weeks
- **Cross-functional alignment** — as topics arise
- **Strategic discussions** — every 4-6 weeks

If a topic hasn't been touched in its cycle, manager should raise it in next 1:1.

## What NOT to do in a 1:1

- Status updates that could be in a written report or async update
- Group decisions (don't make group decisions in 1:1s — surprises the rest of the team)
- Manager monologue (the report should talk 60-70% of the time)
- Cancellations as the default ("I'm busy, let's skip this week")
- Letting it become a meeting where work gets done (book separate meetings for actual project work)
- Bringing up performance issues for the first time in a 1:1 (those need their own dedicated, prepared conversation)

## Special situations

**The 1:1 with a new direct report (first 30 days):**
- 60+ minutes, not 30
- Cover: how they like to work, communication preferences, motivations, concerns
- Set expectations for the 1:1 cadence
- Less coaching, more listening

**The 1:1 with a struggling report:**
- Don't avoid the hard topic; raise it explicitly
- Concrete examples; impact; expectation; agreement on what changes
- Document the conversation
- Don't slip the corrective feedback into a casual 1:1

**The 1:1 with a high performer who might leave:**
- Explicit "stay" conversation periodically: "What would make this the best job you've ever had? What might cause you to leave?"
- The answers often surprise; act on what you hear

**The 1:1 with a CEO or senior exec (as a report):**
- Bring 3-5 things you've already thought about
- Bring decisions you're navigating; ask for input
- Don't show up with no agenda — the CEO's most valuable input is on YOUR most important questions

## Discipline

- **Cancel only when truly necessary.** Repeated cancellations signal "you're not a priority."
- **Show up prepared.** Both sides.
- **Take notes; follow through.** The running doc is the artifact that makes the relationship operational.
- **Ask for feedback regularly.** "What's one thing I could do to make me a better manager for you?"
- **Be willing to be wrong.** The 1:1 is a place for honesty in both directions.

## Closing summary

End the template with a reminder: the 1:1 is the most under-leveraged tool in management. Most companies have weak 1:1 culture. Building it deliberately is one of the highest-ROI investments an executive can make.
