# Monthly Business Review (MBR)

An MBR is a forcing function for honest assessment of how the business is performing against plan. The document is more important than the meeting — writing it surfaces what's actually happening; the meeting just confirms decisions.

## Inputs to gather first

Before drafting, confirm or ask for:

1. **The reporting month and YTD period**
2. **Actuals vs. budget for revenue and major opex lines**
3. **Top KPIs the team tracks** — ARR, NRR, CAC, pipeline, headcount, etc.
4. **The 2-3 most consequential things that happened this month** — major wins, losses, hires, departures, product launches
5. **Known decisions the leadership team must make** — hiring, pricing, strategy, budget

If any of these is missing, stop and ask. An MBR without actuals or context produces an empty template.

## Output structure

Six sections, in this order. Total length: 4-6 pages.

### 1. Executive Summary (1 page)

The 3-5 things leadership most needs to know coming into this MBR. The CEO's POV on the state of the business this month.

Format: 3-5 bullet points, each one specific.
- Headline metric and trajectory ("ARR ended at $14.2M, +6% MoM, +52% YoY, in line with plan")
- Most important development of the month
- Most important problem or risk surfaced
- The decision or topic the team needs to align on

### 4. Strategic Initiatives Update (1 page)

Status of the top 3-5 strategic initiatives (often the OKRs).

Per initiative:
- **Initiative**: name and brief description
- **Status**: 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴
- **Progress**: 1-2 sentences on what happened this month
- **Risks / blockers**: anything that could change status
- **Decision needed**: yes/no

### 5. Decisions and Asks (½-1 page)

Specific decisions the leadership team needs to make in this MBR:

For each decision:
- **What we're deciding**: clear, specific
- **The recommendation**: what the function head recommends
- **The trade-offs**: who's on each side, what the alternatives are
- **The decision-maker**: who owns the final call

If there are no decisions needed, say so explicitly. Don't manufacture asks to fill space.

### 6. Looking Ahead (½ page)

The 2-3 things the leadership team should be focused on next month:
- The biggest risk to the plan
- The biggest opportunity to lean into
- The most important upcoming milestone

## Tone

- Direct. No marketing voice.
- Numbers, not adjectives.
- Surface bad news in section 1; don't bury it on page 5.
- Recommendations, not just observations.
- Document leadership's actual point of view, not the consensus-safe version.

## Discipline

- **Refresh the template, not just the numbers.** Last month's commentary is rarely this month's.
- **Variance analysis is the work.** Tables alone don't tell the story.
- **Write the document first; meeting second.** If the document is good, the meeting is short.
- **Distribute 24-48 hours before the meeting.** Pre-read discipline.
- **Capture decisions in the meeting itself**; circulate within 24 hours.

## What NOT to include

- Slide-style headers and bullets without substance
- Activity reports ("the team had 47 customer meetings this month")
- Self-congratulation without metric backing
- Vague commitments ("we'll focus on growth next month") without specifics
- Anything the leadership team already knows — repeat only what's actionable

## Closing summary

End the MBR with one sentence: the single most important thing the leadership team should be paying attention to this month.
