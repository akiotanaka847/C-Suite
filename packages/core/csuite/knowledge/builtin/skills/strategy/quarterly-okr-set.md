# Quarterly OKR Set

OKRs operationalize strategy. The exercise of writing them is at least as valuable as the resulting document.

## Inputs to gather first

Before drafting anything, confirm or ask for:

1. **Annual / multi-quarter direction** — what are the company-level OKRs or strategic priorities this period rolls up to
2. **Team or scope of OKRs** — are these company OKRs, team OKRs, or function OKRs
3. **Last quarter's outcomes** — what shipped, what didn't, what carried over
4. **Known constraints** — capacity, hiring plan, dependencies on other teams
5. **The 1-2 biggest bets the team is making** — the strategic moves that need to show up in OKRs

If any of these is missing, stop and ask. OKRs without context produce a wish list, not priorities.

## Output structure

Produce 3-5 Objectives, each with 3-5 Key Results.

### Per Objective

- **Objective** — qualitative, inspirational, achievable in 90 days, unambiguous
  - Good: "Establish the company as the default option for mid-market RevOps teams"
  - Bad: "Grow the business" / "Launch 5 features"

- **Why it matters this quarter** — 1-2 sentences connecting the objective to company strategy or current circumstances

### Per Key Result

- **Key Result** — measurable outcome with specific number and deadline
  - Good: "Net Revenue Retention reaches 115% by end of Q3"
  - Bad: "Improve customer success process" (task, not outcome)

- **Owner** — single named person accountable
- **Target value** — specific number to hit
- **Baseline** — current value (so progress is measurable)
- **Confidence** — High / Medium / Low at the time of setting

## Discipline

- **3-5 Objectives max**. More than that = no real priorities.
- **3-5 KRs per Objective**. Fewer = under-defined; more = diluted.
- **70% achievement is success**. If KRs feel comfortable to hit at 100%, raise the bar.
- **KRs measure outcomes, not effort**. "Launched feature X" is not a KR; "feature X drives 20% of new signups" is.
- **No more than 2 KRs per person**. People can't deeply own more than that.
- **Objectives should require trade-offs**. If the team can hit all OKRs without giving anything up, they're not strategic — they're operational.

## After the draft — the validation pass

For each Objective, ask:
1. Does this connect to the company / strategy level above?
2. Does this require us to do something different from BAU?
3. Will hitting this materially move our business 90 days from now?

For each Key Result, ask:
1. Is the number specific and measurable?
2. Is there a single accountable owner?
3. Are we 70% confident — i.e., requires real effort but achievable?
4. Will leadership recognize this as a real outcome at quarter end?

If any answer is weak, revise before finalizing.

## What NOT to include

- More than 5 Objectives
- KRs that are tasks ("launch X", "hire Y", "build Z" without an outcome metric)
- Recurring operational metrics dressed up as KRs ("maintain SLA at 99%" is a guardrail, not an OKR)
- Goals you're 95%+ confident in (sandbagging)
- Goals you're 30%- confident in (aspirational; demoralizing)
- Compensation language (OKRs and bonus targets are separate conversations; conflating corrupts both)

## Closing summary

End with three sentences:

1. The single most important Objective and why
2. The biggest trade-off the team is making this quarter (what they're explicitly NOT doing)
3. The one signal that, if it changes, would force re-prioritization
