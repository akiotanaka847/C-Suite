# Compensation Design

Compensation is one of the highest-leverage and most-misused tools in management. Done well, it aligns behavior with strategy without becoming the conversation. Done poorly, it becomes the only conversation.

## The Total Rewards Stack

A complete comp philosophy addresses five components:

1. **Base salary** — predictable cash for the steady-state job
2. **Variable cash** — bonus, commissions, spot rewards tied to outcomes
3. **Equity** — long-term ownership aligning with company outcomes
4. **Benefits** — health, retirement, insurance, leave, perks
5. **Non-cash** — career growth, learning, flexibility, status, mission

Companies that compete only on (1) and (4) lose to companies that compete on the whole stack.

## Compensation Philosophy — Decide These Five Things First

Before designing the system, the leadership team must agree on:

1. **Target market percentile** — do you pay at the 50th, 75th, or 90th percentile of market? Higher percentile costs more cash but recruits and retains better. Most growth-stage companies target 60-75th.
2. **Cash vs. equity mix** — does the philosophy lean cash-rich (later-stage, mature) or equity-rich (early-stage, big-upside)?
3. **Variable vs. base mix** — sales typically 50/50, executives 70/30 base/variable, engineering typically 90/10 or 100/0
4. **Geo strategy** — pay by location of work (more equitable, simpler), by location of HQ (more competitive but expensive remote), or hybrid
5. **Transparency** — open bands (Buffer model), private bands shared on request, or fully opaque

These decisions cascade into every offer. Inconsistent answers across hiring managers = chaos.

## Pay Bands and Leveling

A leveling framework defines the scope and impact expectations at each level (IC1 through IC8, or M1 through M4 for managers). Each level has a pay band: minimum, midpoint, maximum.

**Why level matters more than title:**
- Title inflation is free; level inflation costs money
- Promotions should require demonstrated scope at the new level, not just tenure
- "Senior" at a startup is "Mid" at a FAANG — leveling forces precision

**Band design:**
- Bands typically span 30-50% (max is 1.3-1.5x min)
- Adjacent bands should overlap by 15-25% — a senior IC4 should be paid more than a junior IC5
- Cost-of-living differentials handled as zone adjustments, not as separate bands

**Refreshing bands:**
- Refresh annually using market data (Radford, Pave, OptionImpact, Carta Total Comp)
- Don't refresh in response to a single offer war — that distorts equity across the team

## Equity Design

Equity is the most-misunderstood and most-litigated form of comp. Done right, it's strategic alignment. Done wrong, it's a recruiting myth that disappoints on exit.

**Standard practices:**
- 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (25% at year 1, monthly thereafter)
- Stock options (NSOs or ISOs) for early-stage; RSUs once the company can pay tax withholding
- ISOs only for employees, capped at $100k vesting per year (excess converts to NSOs automatically)
- 10-year exercise window from grant; 90-day post-termination window is standard (but increasingly replaced with 5-10 year windows as the modern norm)

**Grant sizing principles:**
- Tied to level, not negotiation skill
- Refresh grants annually for key talent — early grants get diluted by subsequent rounds; refreshes maintain alignment
- New hires get a larger initial grant than refreshes — this is the "ownership stake" of joining
- Executives get larger grants — both for impact-level alignment and because cash-comp gap to senior IC is smaller than equity gap

**Common equity mistakes:**
- Promising "0.5% equity" without specifying pre or post option pool, fully-diluted vs. issued — almost certain to cause a fight
- Granting at outdated 409A valuations — when the next round repricesgreatly, options can be underwater within months
- No refresh program — the people who joined earliest end up with less than new hires after 4-5 years
- Hidden cliffs and acceleration that nobody understands until termination

## Variable Pay Design

**Sales commission plans:**
- Quota-based with accelerators above quota (e.g., 1x rate to quota, 1.5x rate above)
- On-target earnings (OTE) is base + commission at 100% quota; typically 50/50 split for AEs
- SPIFs (spot bonuses) for specific motions (new logos, multi-year deals, target accounts) — use sparingly to avoid distortion
- Clawbacks for churn within 6-12 months — aligns AE with retention

**Executive bonus plans:**
- Tied to 3-5 quantitative goals (revenue, EBITDA, key product or strategic milestones)
- Avoid more than 5 metrics — they cancel each other out
- Annual payout for execs; quarterly distorts toward short-term

**Non-sales bonus plans:**
- Often more trouble than they're worth at startups
- If you do them, tie to company performance (not individual) — otherwise you've recreated a sales comp plan for non-sales people

## Compensation Reviews

**Annual cycle:**
1. Refresh market data (vendor reports, internal market scans)
2. Calibrate bands and update if needed
3. Managers propose changes for each report (raise %, equity refresh, promotion if level change)
4. Leadership calibration — review across managers, fix outliers
5. Communicate to individuals

**Adjustment principles:**
- Inflation/market adjustments first (the baseline)
- Performance differentiation on top — top performers get 1.5-2x the cycle average
- Promotion-level adjustments are separate from annual cycle and can happen anytime (but justify the off-cycle reason)
- Equity refreshes for key talent and high performers

**Compression** — when new hires get paid more than tenured employees doing the same job. The most corrosive comp pattern. Fix proactively with off-cycle adjustments; do not wait for the tenured employee to ask.

## When Compensation Is the Wrong Lever

Many "compensation problems" are actually:
- Manager problem (employee feels undervalued because they don't know what they're contributing)
- Career growth problem (no path visible, so they shop the market)
- Role design problem (job has expanded but title/scope/comp haven't kept up)
- Strategy problem (whole function is undervalued because leadership doesn't think it's important)

Throwing comp at any of these papers over the underlying issue for 6-12 months. Diagnose the cause before adjusting the price.
