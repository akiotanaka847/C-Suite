# Performance Management

## The Performance Conversation Framework

Most managers avoid performance conversations because they lack structure. Use this:

**Setup**: "I want to talk about [specific behavior/outcome]. I want to share what I'm seeing and understand your perspective."

**Observation**: Describe the specific behavior or result, not a character judgment. "In the last 3 sprints, the features shipped had more bugs than our target quality bar — 14 P1 bugs vs. our target of 0." Not: "You're not detail-oriented."

**Impact**: Connect the behavior to a business outcome. "This has caused 3 customer escalations and the support team has had to deprioritize other work."

**Question**: Understand their perspective. "What's your read on what's happening?" Often there are systemic causes (unclear requirements, technical debt) you don't know about.

**Agreement**: Reach explicit agreement on what changes. "So we agree: going forward, all features go through a QA checklist before marking complete. You'll lead defining that checklist by Friday. Does that work?"

**Follow-up**: Set a specific check-in date. "Let's revisit in 4 weeks."

Document this conversation. Not because you plan to fire them, but because memory is unreliable and documentation protects both parties.

## Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

A PIP is appropriate when:
- You have clearly communicated the performance gap
- The employee has had a reasonable opportunity to improve
- You believe improvement is possible (if not, don't PIP — it's not fair to them)

PIP structure:
1. **Statement of concern**: Specific, documented, measurable. "In Q2, quota attainment was 52%. The target is 80%."
2. **Improvement targets**: Specific, measurable, achievable in the timeline. "By end of Q3: achieve 75% quota attainment, maintain pipeline at 3x quota."
3. **Support**: What the company will provide. Coaching, resources, manager time.
4. **Timeline**: Typically 30-90 days. Shorter for clear-cut situations.
5. **Consequences**: Explicit. "If targets are not met, employment will be terminated."

Before issuing a PIP, consult with HR and legal. Employment law varies by jurisdiction, and the documentation and process matter.

## Managing Involuntary Separations

The most common mistake: delaying too long. Every week of delay:
- Costs the company salary for a non-contributing employee
- Demoralizes the team who is watching and drawing conclusions
- Deepens the performance problem
- Makes the conversation more difficult

When you've decided to separate:
1. Legal and HR review first — ensure process was followed, documentation is in order
2. Have the conversation on a Monday or Tuesday, not Friday (gives them time to reach their support network, consult a lawyer, start job search)
3. Keep it short, clear, and kind. "We've decided to make a change and today is your last day." Don't over-explain or re-litigate.
4. Have severance and logistics ready to discuss immediately
5. Be prepared for emotion — give the person space and dignity

The team will find out. Communicate transparently (within legal limits): "We've made a change on the team. I'm not able to share details, but I want you to know [team impact and next steps]." Don't let the rumor mill fill the vacuum.

## Compensation and Performance

**Avoid**: Tying OKR scores to compensation. It corrupts the OKR process (people sandbag goals).

**Do**: Use compensation reviews to reward sustained high performance and market-adjust. Separate the conversation from OKRs.

**Performance rating systems**: Controversial. Forced distribution ("stack ranking") drives unhealthy competition. Calibration sessions among managers (without a forced distribution) tend to produce more accurate assessments and build manager capability.

## High-Performer Retention

The cost of losing an A-player:
- 6-12 months of recruitment time
- 3-6 months of ramp for the replacement
- Knowledge transfer loss
- Team morale impact

Retention drivers (ranked):
1. Interesting and challenging work
2. Strong manager relationship
3. Career growth opportunities
4. Compensation (needs to be competitive, but rarely the top driver)
5. Mission and values alignment

Have explicit "stay conversations" with A-players: "What would make this the best job you've ever had?" and "What would cause you to consider leaving?" The answers will surprise you.
