# Culture and Values

Culture is what people do when leadership isn't watching. Values are what leadership says they want them to do. The gap between the two is where culture problems live.

## What Culture Actually Is

A working definition: **culture is the set of behaviors that get rewarded, tolerated, and punished in your company.**

- Values posters are not culture
- The all-hands speech is not culture
- The mission statement is not culture
- Who you promote, who you fire, what you spend money on, what you tolerate in meetings — that is culture

If your stated values say "transparency" but the company answers hard questions with corporate non-statements, your actual value is "managed messaging." Employees calibrate to actual values, not stated ones, within 60 days of joining.

## Designing Values That Matter

Bad values are platitudes everyone agrees with ("integrity," "excellence," "teamwork"). Good values are choices — they specify what you prioritize when prioritization is hard.

**Test for good values:**
1. Could a competitor credibly claim the opposite? If not, it's a platitude. ("We value integrity" — who would claim they don't?)
2. Does it specify trade-offs? ("Speed over perfection" implies you'll ship imperfect things; "perfection over speed" implies you'll miss windows)
3. Does it predict a specific behavior? ("Customer obsession" should mean specific things you do that others don't)

**Examples of good values:**
- Netflix: "We hire and retain only A-players. Adequate performance gets a generous severance." (Implies specific HR behavior)
- Stripe: "Move with urgency and focus." (Specifies what to do under uncertainty)
- Amazon: "Disagree and commit." (Specifies decision behavior)

**Examples of weak values:**
- "We are passionate." (So is everyone)
- "Customer first." (Everyone says this)
- "Innovation." (Doesn't tell you what to do)

## How Culture Actually Gets Built

Three mechanisms — in order of impact:

**1. Hiring and firing decisions** (highest impact)
- Who you hire signals what you value
- Who you fire signals what you won't tolerate
- Failure to fire bad-fit high performers signals that performance trumps values
- The fastest way to corrupt culture: keep a "brilliant jerk" because they hit numbers

**2. What leadership pays attention to** (high impact)
- What gets reviewed in board meetings, what gets celebrated in all-hands, what gets corrected in 1:1s
- If leadership talks about quality every meeting and ships buggy releases, the talk is noise
- If leadership talks about diversity once a year, the talk is noise

**3. Organizational rituals** (medium-high impact)
- How meetings start, how decisions get communicated, how new hires get onboarded
- Friday demos signal a shipping culture; weekly status meetings signal a reporting culture
- Onboarding determines what new hires think the company values for their first year

Lower-impact mechanisms (offsites, posters, swag) are noise unless the high-impact mechanisms reinforce them.

## Cultural Hiring — Beyond "Culture Fit"

"Culture fit" is dangerous shorthand that often justifies hiring people who are demographically similar to the existing team. Replace with two separate concepts:

**Values fit** — does the candidate behave in ways that match your stated values? Assessed through behavioral questions tied to each value.
- For "speed over perfection": "Tell me about a time you shipped something you knew wasn't perfect. How did you decide it was good enough?"

**Add, not fit** — does the candidate bring something the team is missing? Different background, different functional experience, different perspective?

Hiring for values-fit + culture-add produces diverse teams aligned on behavior. Hiring for "culture fit" produces homogeneous teams aligned on background.

## Cultural Erosion — How It Happens

Cultures don't change because of one bad decision. They erode through a thousand small ones.

**Pattern 1: Tolerated bad behavior from high performers**
- "She brings in 30% of revenue, we can't fire her even if she's destroying the team"
- Outcome: the rest of the team learns that values are conditional. Best people leave. Culture is now "performance excuses everything."

**Pattern 2: Diluted hiring bar in growth phases**
- "We need 50 engineers by Q3, we can't be as picky"
- Outcome: every hire below bar takes more management time and signals a lower bar to existing employees. Bar drops permanently.

**Pattern 3: Leadership saying one thing, doing another**
- "We value transparency" + leadership having private off-sites where strategy is decided and then announced
- Outcome: employees stop believing stated values; they navigate by the unstated ones

**Pattern 4: Process accretion**
- Each problem solved by adding a new approval, meeting, or template
- Outcome: bureaucratic culture, the people who liked the early culture leave

**Pattern 5: Acquisitions without integration discipline**
- Bring in a 50-person company with different working norms; let them keep their norms
- Outcome: two cultures within one company; neither is now what it was

## Cultural Repair

Once culture has eroded, repair is slow and painful. Options in order of severity:

1. **Recommit and demonstrate** — leadership names the gap explicitly and changes their own behavior visibly for 6-12 months
2. **Personnel changes** — remove the most visible violators of the desired culture, including senior people
3. **Process surgery** — kill the bureaucratic process that calcified the wrong behaviors
4. **Structural change** — reorg to break apart the subcultures that diverged

Most cultural repair attempts fail because they stop at (1). Leadership announces a renewed commitment, runs a values workshop, and nothing structural changes.

## Cultural Health Metrics

Soft, but better than nothing:

- **eNPS (employee NPS)** — quarterly, trended; absolute value matters less than direction
- **Voluntary regrettable attrition rate** — high performers leaving is the alarm
- **Manager effectiveness scores** (Glassdoor-style 360s for managers) — managers create or destroy local culture
- **Time-to-hire for senior roles** — if cycle times are growing, your reputation in market is shifting

Track these. Don't optimize them — they're symptoms, not levers. The levers are the high-impact mechanisms above.

## A Final Sharp Question

Ask your leadership team to answer independently, in writing:

*"What gets you fired here? Name three behaviors."*

If you don't get the same answer from at least 80% of the team, your culture isn't actually defined — it's interpreted differently by every leader. That's a culture problem disguised as a values question.
