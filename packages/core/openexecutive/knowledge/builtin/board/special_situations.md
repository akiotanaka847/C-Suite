# Board Special Situations

Regular board meetings are quarterly cadence; special situations are when governance gets tested. The companies that handle these well survive intact. The ones that don't lose executives, investors, and sometimes the company itself.

## Down Rounds

A financing round at a lower valuation than the prior round. Common in market corrections; emotionally difficult; often necessary.

**Pre-decision board work:**
- Honest assessment of alternatives: bridge financing, cost cuts, strategic acquisition, recap
- Cash flow analysis under each scenario
- Modeling of dilution under different round sizes and valuations
- Pre-discussion with major existing investors about willingness to participate

**The board conversation:**
- Frame the choice: raise at lower valuation vs. run out of cash vs. distress sale
- Don't relitigate why the prior valuation was set — water under the bridge
- Focus on: what raise size enables what milestones, what dilution acceptable, what governance changes acceptable

**Mechanical considerations:**
- Anti-dilution adjustments (existing investors get more shares to compensate for valuation drop)
- Option pool refresh (employees often re-granted to offset dilution)
- Employee equity messaging (refreshes, communication, retention)
- Communication to existing investors not participating (acknowledge dilution; explain why round is necessary)

**Common down-round mistakes:**
- Waiting too long (cash runs to 3 months; no leverage)
- Insider-only round at minimal valuation (signals to outside investors that no third party would lead)
- Over-dilutive structure (recap that wipes out common; founders and employees walk)
- Poor employee comms (rumor mill fills the vacuum)

## M&A Approval

When the company is being acquired or making a transformative acquisition. The board's role intensifies.

**Selling the company:**

*Board's fiduciary duty:* maximize value for shareholders (especially common in liquidation scenarios)

*Process discipline:*
- Engagement of investment bankers (sometimes optional for tech deals)
- Formal sale process (broader auction) vs. negotiated deal with single buyer
- Special committee of independent directors if conflicts exist (e.g., investor with secondary interest)
- Fairness opinion from financial advisor (becomes important if challenged)

*Board calls:*
- Whether to engage with an inbound offer at all
- Whether to run a process
- Which bidders to engage
- Final terms acceptance
- Allocation of consideration (preferred preferences, common, employee)

**Buying a significant company:**

*Board approval typically required for:*
- Deals above a defined threshold (often $10-50M+)
- Stock-based consideration affecting cap table
- Deals requiring debt financing
- Strategic transformations

*Board work:*
- Review of strategic rationale
- Diligence findings (financial, commercial, legal, technical)
- Integration plan
- Synergy projections (with realistic discount)
- Approval with conditions if appropriate

**Conflict situations:**
- Investor on board pushing for sale to fund their own returns
- Investor with stake in potential acquirer
- Founder-CEO with strong opinion about future ownership

*Procedural protections:*
- Recusal of conflicted directors from votes
- Special committee of independent directors
- Independent financial and legal advisors

## CEO Transitions

The most significant board decision and often the most-mishandled.

**Founder CEO transitions:**

Common patterns:
- Founder steps to chairman/CTO; new CEO recruited
- Founder steps to product role; new CEO leads operations
- Founder departs entirely (rare; usually contentious)

*Board work:*
- Honest assessment of whether the founder is the right CEO for current stage
- Conversation with the founder (not behind their back)
- Search for successor (internal vs. external)
- Transition plan (timeline, role definition, communication)
- Equity treatment for departing or transitioning founder

**Performance-driven CEO change:**

*Triggers:*
- Sustained underperformance against plan
- Loss of board confidence in execution
- Strategic misalignment that can't be bridged
- Inability to recruit and retain leadership team
- Specific incidents (governance failure, conduct issue)

*Process:*
- Lead independent director leads the conversation
- Board executive session without the CEO
- Performance documentation reviewed
- Decision communicated to the CEO personally before announcement
- Severance and equity treatment finalized

**Succession planning:**
- Every board should have a current view on CEO succession
- Internal candidates identified and developed
- External landscape monitored
- "If the CEO is hit by a bus tomorrow, who's the interim?" — a documented answer

**Communication:**
- All-hands announcement (CEO presence helpful when amicable; explicit absence if not)
- Customer comms for top accounts
- Investor comms (especially for fundraising or M&A timing)
- Press / external (often required for public or growth-stage companies)

## Executive Compensation Decisions

Board-level (especially compensation committee) decisions on senior executive comp.

**Annual comp review:**
- CEO comp set by compensation committee (full board ratification)
- Other executive comp recommended by CEO, reviewed by comp committee
- Benchmarking to peer companies
- Performance assessment vs. goals
- Pay-for-performance discipline

**Equity grants:**
- New executive hires (sign-on grants)
- Refresh grants for retention
- Performance-based equity (PSUs, milestone-vesting)

**Pay-for-performance discipline:**
- Defined performance metrics with thresholds
- Documented rationale for compensation decisions
- Public-company implications (CD&A disclosure, say-on-pay)

**Common compensation governance failures:**
- CEO sets own comp without committee oversight
- Comp not tied to performance; pays out regardless
- Peer benchmarking gamed (cherry-picked comparables that justify higher pay)
- Insider transactions (executive loans, related-party deals)

## Crisis Governance

Major reputational or financial events: data breach, executive misconduct, regulatory action, financial restatement.

**Crisis structure:**
- Crisis team named (CEO, GC, CFO, communications lead, board chair)
- Lead independent director engaged early
- External counsel (potentially separate from regular counsel for independence)
- External communications counsel
- Reporting cadence (often daily) to full board

**Specific crisis types:**

*Executive misconduct:*
- Investigation by independent party
- Interim measures (leave, separation) during investigation
- Audit and comp committee involvement
- Board executive session for decisions
- External communication only after investigation provides factual basis

*Data breach:*
- Notifications per regulatory and contractual obligations
- Customer communication
- Forensic investigation
- Remediation plan
- Board involvement in major incidents (regular updates to full board; audit committee for ongoing oversight)

*Financial restatement:*
- Audit committee leadership
- External auditor engagement
- SEC notification (public companies)
- Press / investor communication
- Often leads to executive consequences (CFO change at minimum, sometimes CEO)

**The board's role:**
- Oversight (not management) of the response
- Decisions on consequences for individuals
- Communications oversight
- External engagement (regulators, key investors, sometimes press)

## Activist Investors

A shareholder takes a public position pushing for changes (board composition, strategy, capital allocation, sale of company).

**For private companies (rare):**
- Often a major investor pushing for liquidity or strategic change
- Negotiated rather than public

**For public companies (common):**
- Public letters to board and shareholders
- Proxy contests (running competing slate of directors)
- Demanded changes: buybacks, dividends, divestitures, sale of company

**Board response framework:**
- Engage seriously (engagement is mandatory; dismissal usually escalates)
- Independent assessment of activist's points (some may be right)
- Strategic review (often public) of company direction
- Communication to all shareholders (not just activist)
- Defensive measures (poison pill) used judiciously and disclosed appropriately

## Whistleblower / Internal Complaints

Material complaints about company conduct (financial reporting, harassment, regulatory violation).

**Process:**
- Audit committee oversight (especially for financial complaints)
- Independent investigation (often outside counsel)
- Whistleblower protections (no retaliation; documented)
- Findings reported to board
- Corrective action taken
- External notification if material

**Common failures:**
- Investigating the whistleblower instead of the allegation
- Retaliation (even subtle) that compounds the original complaint
- Cover-up that's later exposed
- Lack of independence in the investigation

## Board Conflicts

When directors disagree materially.

**Healthy conflict:**
- Different perspectives surfaced before decisions
- Vigorous debate in executive session
- Unified position once decided
- Continued constructive engagement

**Unhealthy conflict:**
- Personal animosity
- Public dissent (leaks, contrary press statements)
- Director withdrawing engagement
- Bloc voting along lines that ignore merits

**Resolution paths:**
- Chair or lead independent mediates
- Smaller subset works through the issue
- Board self-evaluation surfaces patterns
- In extreme cases: director departures or special committee formation

## Board Member Departures

Sometimes necessary, always delicate.

**Departure scenarios:**
- Term ending (natural departure if term-limited)
- Performance or attendance issues
- Conflict of interest emergence
- Strategic mismatch with company stage
- Personal reasons (health, time)

**Process:**
- Chair or lead independent has the conversation
- Frame around evolving needs (vs. personal critique)
- Allow graceful exit ("not standing for re-election", "stepping back to focus on new role")
- Maintain relationship — former directors often stay valuable

**Replacement:**
- Identify capability gaps
- Search via existing board network, then formal recruiting if needed
- Reference and diligence as for any senior hire
- Onboard properly (briefings, materials, time)

## The Special-Situation Diagnostic

When entering any special situation:
1. Have we engaged appropriate independent counsel and advisors?
2. Are conflicts of interest identified and managed (recusal, special committee)?
3. What's the timeline and what milestones drive it?
4. Who are the affected parties and how will they be communicated with?
5. What's the documented decision trail — would it withstand scrutiny later?

If any answer is weak, slow down and address it. Most board litigation comes from special situations where process was rushed or shortcuts were taken.
