# Competitive Teardown

A competitive teardown is not a feature comparison chart. It is a strategic read on a company.

## Section 1: Who they are (3-5 sentences)

- Founded when, by whom, last funding round and amount
- Their stated mission and the underlying market hypothesis
- Where they sit in the value chain (incumbent disruptor, adjacent expander, new category creator)

## Section 2: Positioning

- The customer pain they claim to own
- Their three highest-leverage messages (from website, sales decks, recent talks)
- The customer they sell BEST to — by ICP, not generic persona

## Section 3: Go-to-market

- Primary motion: PLG / sales-led / channel / hybrid
- Pricing model and 1-2 anchor prices (per-seat, usage, platform fee)
- Sales team size and structure if knowable (LinkedIn headcount filtered for AE/SE titles)
- Their best-known channel partnerships or integrations

## Section 4: Product

- Three core capabilities they lead with
- What they obviously DON'T do (the negative space matters as much as the positive)
- Most recent shipped feature that signals their roadmap direction

## Section 5: Threat assessment

This is the most important section. Rate the threat in three dimensions:

- **Direct deal threat**: how often do we lose deals to them, and which segments? (1-5)
- **Strategic threat**: are they moving into our adjacent expansion zones? (1-5)
- **Talent threat**: are we losing hires to them, or are they recruiting our team? (1-5)

## Section 6: What to do about it

End with three recommendations, ranked by urgency:
1. **This quarter** — one concrete action: messaging shift, product gap to close, account-list defense
2. **This year** — one durable bet: differentiation we lean harder into, or a market segment to wall off
3. **Watch list** — one signal that, if it changes, would force us to re-do this entire analysis

No bullet points longer than two sentences. No "they have great UX" without specifics. No fabricated stats — if you don't know a number, say so.
