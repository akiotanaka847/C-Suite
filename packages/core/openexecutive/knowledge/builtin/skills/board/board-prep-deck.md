# Board Prep Deck

A board deck is not a status update. It is a structured artifact that lets the board efficiently exercise oversight, contribute strategic input, and make required decisions. Most decks fail because they substitute information density for clarity.

(See also `board/board_communication.md` for general principles and `skills/board/board-update-memo.md` for the monthly written update.)

## Inputs to gather first

Before drafting, confirm or ask for:

1. **The meeting context** — quarterly board meeting? Special board meeting? Annual planning meeting?
2. **Financial actuals** — for the most recent reporting period and YTD
3. **Operating metrics** — top KPIs with current vs. prior period
4. **The 2-3 strategic topics** the CEO wants board input on
5. **Required decisions / approvals** — equity grants, material contracts, financing, etc.
6. **Recent material developments** — wins, losses, executive changes, customer concentration shifts

If any of these is missing, stop and ask. A board deck written without these inputs is a template, not a decision tool.

## Output structure

A standard 2-hour board meeting deck has 6 sections totaling 30-50 slides (slides should be lightweight; decks read more than presented).

### Section 1: Executive Summary (3-5 slides)

The CEO's point of view on the state of the business, written before the rest of the deck.

**Slide 1 — Headline (one slide)**
- The 3-4 things the board most needs to understand
- Bullets, not paragraphs
- "We're [on / ahead of / behind] plan because [X]. The most important question for this meeting is [Y]."

**Slide 2 — Wins (one slide)**
- 3-5 specific wins in the period
- Customers named (with permission), metrics specific, impact tangible

**Slide 3 — Concerns and Risks (one slide)**
- 3-5 specific things that aren't working
- Direct, not euphemistic
- Each with a brief on what's being done

**Slide 4 — Asks (one slide)**
- What the CEO needs from the board this meeting
- Decisions to make, intros to make, advice to give

### Section 2: Business Update (8-12 slides)

The "what happened in the quarter" section. Financial and operating.

**Financial performance:**
- Revenue (recognized + ARR) actuals vs. budget
- Major opex lines actuals vs. budget
- Variance commentary (what changed and why)
- Cash position and runway

**Key metrics dashboard:**
- 8-12 KPIs with trend
- For each: this period, prior period, vs. plan, trailing trend

**Customer / commercial:**
- New ARR composition
- Churned ARR composition
- NRR / GRR (TTM)
- Pipeline coverage trend
- Top deals won and lost (with reason)

**People:**
- Headcount end of period
- Open roles and pipeline
- Notable hires and departures
- Comp / equity refreshes if relevant

Keep this section data-dense but not endless. The board can read; they don't need narration.

### Section 3: Deep Dive Topic 1 (8-12 slides)

A pre-selected strategic topic where board input is genuinely valuable.

Structure:
- **The question** (1 slide) — what specifically does the board need to weigh in on
- **The context** (2-3 slides) — what we know, where we've been
- **The options** (2-3 slides) — typically 2-4 paths
- **The recommendation** (1-2 slides) — leadership's view, with rationale
- **The trade-offs** (1 slide) — what we lose under each option
- **The decision needed** (1 slide) — what we're asking the board to approve, advise, or discuss

Common topics:
- M&A opportunity assessment
- Major strategic pivot or expansion
- Fundraising timing and structure
- Significant org change
- Pricing or business-model change
- Major customer concentration response

### Section 4: Deep Dive Topic 2 (8-12 slides)

Second strategic topic. Same structure as Section 3.

Most board meetings should have 2 deep-dive topics. Less than 2 wastes the board; more than 2 dilutes attention.

### Section 5: Decisions and Approvals (3-5 slides)

Items requiring formal board approval.

For each:
- **What's being approved** — concise description
- **The numbers / terms** — specific
- **The recommendation** — leadership's recommendation, often with rationale
- **The voting** — captured in board minutes after the meeting

Typical items:
- Equity grants for new hires (especially executives)
- Stock plan amendments
- Material contracts above board-approval threshold
- Annual budget approval
- Operating plan approval
- Significant capital commitments
- M&A LOIs or definitive agreements

### Section 6: Executive Session (no slides; CEO leaves)

The last 15-30 minutes; CEO and other executives exit. Board discusses:
- Management performance
- CEO compensation
- Sensitive personnel matters
- Strategic concerns to surface to leadership later

The CEO should know what the board discussed (a summary from chair / lead independent), but not be in the room.

## The pre-read

The deck goes to the board 5-7 days in advance. The meeting assumes they've read it.

**Pre-read discipline:**
- One PDF; not a sprawl of attachments
- Searchable and structured
- Sized for reading on tablets (clear fonts, not slide-deck-as-document)

**At the meeting:**
- Don't re-read what they already read
- Walk through ONLY the items needing board input
- Spend the most time on the deep dives, not the standard update

## Communicating bad news

If material bad news is in the deck:

- **Call key board members before the meeting**. Don't let them see it for the first time in the deck.
- **Lead the section with the problem**, not the context. "Q2 revenue missed by 22%; here's why and what we're doing." Not 3 slides of context first.
- **Show diagnosis**, not just impact. The board needs to know you understand WHY.
- **Show a plan**, not just the problem. "We don't know yet" is acceptable if you explain the process.

See `board/board_communication.md` for detail on bad-news communication.

## Discipline

- **Write the executive summary LAST.** Once the rest is done, you know what to highlight.
- **Cut ruthlessly.** A 60-slide deck is usually 30 slides of content padded with 30 of redundancy.
- **One owner per slide.** Slides should be accountable to one person who can defend the data.
- **Numbers consistent across slides.** Nothing erodes trust like the same metric showing different values on different slides.
- **Source the data.** "ARR: $14.2M (source: Salesforce, 5/1/2026)" beats "$14.2M ARR".

## What NOT to include

- Slide-style headers without substance ("Q2 was a strong quarter")
- "Quote of the month" or other padding
- Roadmap details for product features (those belong in product reviews)
- Compensation specifics for non-executive employees
- Anyone's salary, in any context, unless approved for disclosure
- Optimistic forecasts without acknowledging assumptions
- "We're committed to execution" without specifics

## Closing summary

End the deck-prep work with three things:

1. The single most important question for the board this meeting
2. The one decision required, or "no decisions this meeting"
3. The one ask of the board (intro, advice, or "nothing this meeting")

If you can't articulate those three after building the deck, the deck isn't yet focused enough. Cut and refocus before sending.
