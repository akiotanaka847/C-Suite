# Layoff Communications Plan

*Note: A communication plan does not substitute for legal review, severance terms, or selection process discipline. Engage employment counsel before any layoff. See also `hr/layoffs_and_rifs.md` and `legal/employment_law_for_managers.md`.*

The day a layoff happens, the company's character is on display. The communications determine whether the team trusts leadership for the next 12 months — or doesn't.

## Inputs to gather first

Before drafting any communication, confirm or ask for:

1. **The business context** — why this is happening (financial, strategic, restructuring)
2. **The scope** — # of affected employees, % of company, affected functions
3. **The severance philosophy** — what's being offered (cash, benefits, equity, outplacement)
4. **The decision-day timing** — date, day of week, time of day
5. **Legal review status** — has employment counsel signed off; any WARN obligations
6. **The future-state narrative** — what's the company after this (strategic direction, leadership, plans)

If any of these is missing, stop and ask. A communication plan written before the underlying decisions are firm produces commitments that change later — which is its own crisis.

## Output structure

Produce six communication artifacts:

### 1. Affected employee conversation script (per manager)

What managers say in the individual notification meetings.

Structure (5-8 minutes):
- *Opening (10 seconds)*: "Thanks for coming in. I have difficult news to share. We're making changes to the team, and your role is being eliminated effective today."
- *Brief context (15 seconds)*: "This is part of a broader [company restructuring / cost reduction]. The decision is final and is not about your performance."
- *What happens now (90 seconds)*: "[HR person] will walk through the details: your severance is [X weeks / months]; healthcare continues through [date]; you'll receive [outplacement / career support]; your equity will be treated as [...]; final logistics [...]."
- *Your immediate steps (30 seconds)*: "We need to start the transition today. [HR] will help with the laptop return, badge, file transfer of any personal items. Your access to systems is being adjusted shortly."
- *Space for them (open-ended)*: "I know this is a lot. What questions do you have?"
- *Close (15 seconds)*: "I'm grateful for what you've done here. I'll do anything I can to help your next step — references, intros, recommendations."

Discipline:
- Manager + HR partner together; HR handles severance details, manager handles human element
- Stay on script during the news; allow space for reactions
- No re-litigation of the decision
- No commitments not pre-approved (about references, future roles, etc.)
- 30-60 minutes per conversation

### 2. All-hands announcement (CEO)

Delivered to the full company within hours of individual notifications.

Structure (10-15 minutes):

*The hardest part — naming it:*
- "Today, we've made the very difficult decision to reduce our team by [X% / N people]."
- "These changes affect [departments / functions], and the people impacted have been notified this morning."

*The why:*
- Honest business context (1-2 paragraphs)
- "This is not because of these individuals' work. They are talented people who contributed to what we've built."
- Avoid: "We're stronger as a company today" (gaslighting)

*The how — severance and support:*
- Severance, healthcare, outplacement summary
- Acknowledge it's not enough — "We can't make this OK, but we want to do right by our colleagues"

*Future-state — what's not changing:*
- The mission, the strategy, the customers we serve
- The people who remain and the work ahead
- 2-3 specific things the company is committed to

*Future-state — what is changing:*
- Strategic shifts in concrete terms
- Resource allocations
- Operating cadence changes

*Honest about the future:*
- "Is there going to be another round?" — answer honestly within what's known
- Don't promise no more cuts if you can't promise no more cuts
- Don't promise more than you know

*Closing:*
- Acknowledge the moment for everyone in the room
- Q&A: be present; don't deflect
- Or commit to a Q&A within 24-48 hours if not now

### 3. Customer communication (per top accounts)

For top customers, account team reach-out within 24 hours.

Components:
- Brief acknowledgment of the change
- Reassurance of account continuity (their AM remains; their service quality unchanged)
- Specific names of who they should call for what
- Offer of a CEO call if the customer wants one
- Don't ask them to comment publicly or share publicly

For long-tail customers: brief email if functional changes affect them; nothing otherwise.

### 4. Press / external statement (if material)

For layoffs >10% of headcount, or any layoff affecting brand visibility.

Components:
- Short, direct acknowledgment
- Business context (consistent with all-hands)
- Number affected
- Severance philosophy (signals respect for departing employees)
- Forward-looking statement (continuity, strategy)

Avoid: defensive framing; spin; over-promising the future.

### 5. Remaining team communication (manager-led, day 2-5)

For each remaining team member, manager 1:1 within a few days:

Topics:
- How they're doing
- Their reaction and concerns
- Their understanding of the future
- Their workload (some work doesn't disappear with the people)
- What they need from the manager

Manager training before this:
- Listen more than talk
- Don't promise things you can't deliver
- Take notes; follow through
- Escalate concerns to leadership

### 6. Internal FAQ (HR-maintained)

The top 20 questions employees will ask, with consistent answers:
- Why now?
- Why this scope?
- How were people selected?
- Is there going to be another round?
- What about open roles — are they still open?
- What about my equity / RSU vesting?
- What about my project / customer / function?
- What about [specific affected colleague]?
- ...

Distributed to all managers; updated as questions emerge.

## Discipline

- **Coordinate timing.** Customer comms can't precede employee notifications; press can't precede all-hands.
- **Single voice.** Same message from every leader; don't let regional / functional variants emerge.
- **Empathy without performance.** Acknowledge the hard reality without theatrical apology.
- **No surprises in the meeting.** Affected employees should not hear about the layoff in the all-hands — they should already have been told individually.
- **Be present.** Leadership doesn't disappear behind closed doors after the announcement.

## What NOT to include

- "We're stronger as a company today" or similar gaslighting
- "These are difficult decisions but the right ones" without explaining why
- Vague references to "macro conditions" if the issue is company-specific underperformance
- Diminishing language about departing colleagues ("we'll all be more focused")
- Promises about no future layoffs unless you can keep them
- Anything HR / legal hasn't reviewed
- Anything you'd be uncomfortable seeing leaked

## Closing summary

End the plan with three sentences:

1. The single most important message: what the company stands for in this moment
2. The biggest risk in execution: where this could go wrong (e.g., leak before notification, manager off-script, customer surprise)
3. The follow-up rhythm: what comms happen at +1 week, +1 month, +3 months
