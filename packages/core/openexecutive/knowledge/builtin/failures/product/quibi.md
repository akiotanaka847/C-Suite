# Quibi Collapse (2020)

## Situation

Quibi (Quick Bites) was a mobile-first streaming platform founded by Jeffrey Katzenberg and led by CEO Meg Whitman. The premise: premium short-form video (episodes under 10 minutes) optimized for mobile viewing, produced with Hollywood production values. Quibi raised $1.75 billion from Disney, NBCUniversal, Goldman Sachs, and other investors before launching. It launched April 6, 2020 — eleven days after COVID-19 lockdowns began across the United States.

## What Happened

Quibi launched to 1.7 million downloads in week one, below projections. By June 2020, it had 2.7 million subscribers against a target of 7.4 million. In October 2020 — six months after launch — Quibi shut down. It returned approximately $350 million to investors. Jeffrey Katzenberg attributed the failure partly to COVID; internal data and industry observers attributed it to fundamental product-market fit failures that the pandemic exposed but did not create.

## Root Cause

Quibi's product hypothesis — that commuters and people waiting in lines would pay $8/month for premium 10-minute episodes on their phones — was structurally invalidated before launch by COVID. But the hypothesis was also wrong independently of COVID. Mobile video consumption was already dominated by free platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram). The short-form format was Quibi's constraint, not consumers'. Paying for short-form required consumers to change behavior (subscribe, pay) while receiving less than free alternatives (no sharing, phone-only, shorter content). The product could not be watched on TV, shared on social media, or embedded anywhere — eliminating the viral growth loops of every competing streaming platform.

## Key Decision Failures

- **Format designed for the creator's thesis, not consumer behavior**: Short-form episodes were a product constraint imposed by Quibi's technology investment, not a format consumers had requested or demonstrated preference for
- **No social sharing**: Content could not be shared outside the app — clips could not be sent, posted, or embedded; this eliminated the primary discovery mechanism for video content in 2020
- **Phone-only was a bug, not a feature**: The "turnstyle" mobile format (rotating between landscape and portrait) was technically impressive but prevented casting to TV; during COVID, no one was consuming content on a phone they couldn't also watch on their television
- **$1.75B raised before validation**: Quibi raised capital at a scale that locked in the product hypothesis rather than allowing pivots; the financial commitments to studios required delivering the product as designed
- **Advertising commitments made on projection**: Quibi sold $150 million in advertising against subscriber projections it did not meet; advertisers requested refunds

## Lessons

1. **Consumer format preferences are revealed by behavior, not declared in research**: Short-form video was consumed in enormous quantities for free on YouTube and TikTok. Adding a paywall and content exclusivity to the format did not create a new category — it created a worse version of existing free alternatives.
2. **Distribution is a product feature**: The most important question for any media product is "how do users discover and share it?" Quibi had no answer. Virality was impossible by design.
3. **Large fundraises can lock in wrong hypotheses**: Raising $1.75B committed Quibi to delivering the product as specified. A smaller initial raise with milestone-based funding would have permitted iteration before the commitments became irreversible.
4. **"Mobile-first" is not a strategy**: Phone-only was a constraint that created friction compared to multiscreen alternatives, not a differentiated user experience. Distribution constraints should be minimized, not built into the product identity.
